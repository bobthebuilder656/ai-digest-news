    Google will pay people to use less electricity so its data centers can use more

    Google has an answer to some of the electricity supply problems that data center construction is causing : It has signed an agreement with energy supplier Voltus to build what it’s calling a 1-gigawatt virtual power plant, expanding on an earlier 100 megawatt deal the two signed earlier this year. This power plant is virtual in the sense that it doesn’t generate any electricity itself, but aggregates so-called flexible grid capacity into one central resource that can be deployed at times of peak demand. This flexible capacity can be in the form of batteries or of load-shedding: asking others to temporarily stop consuming electricity by shutting down industrial processes or adjusting tens of thousands of smart thermostats at once, effectively moving their electricity usage to a time when there is less demand. Voltus operates by offering incentives to companies to reduce their power consumption temporarily, and also offers inducements to electricity resellers that they can pass on to their customers.

    One new AI word a day, explained simply — it adds up.

    AI Alignment

    AI alignment is the effort to make sure an AI system's actual behavior matches what its developers and users truly intend and value, especially as systems become more capable and autonomous. It's similar to raising a very powerful, very literal-minded genie: you have to be extremely careful that what you asked for is actually what you wanted, because a technically-correct-but-unintended interpretation of an instruction can go badly wrong. It covers everything from a chatbot politely refusing a harmful request to long-term research on keeping far more capable future systems safe.
